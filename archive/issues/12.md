# Provo 42: White Bike Plan v42.0.0

Grok

Here’s the full, practical buying path for the project — from the single prototype all the way through to serious bulk.

**1. The single prototype (right now)**  
Forget bulk for the first bike. Just buy one solid used city bike locally for around 80–120 euros. Marktplaats, Facebook Marketplace, or a small local shop is fine. Look for a straightforward Gazelle, Batavus, Sparta, Cortina or similar steel-frame city bike in rideable condition. You don’t need perfection — just something that rolls, brakes, and can take paint and a permanent mark. Pay cash or instant transfer, pick it up the same day if possible, and treat it as the pure test article. No negotiation drama needed at this scale. Once it has paint, the permanent ID, the combo lock, the Zenodo record, and has spent real time at Science Park, you’ll know exactly what “good enough” looks like for the next step.

**2. The first real batch: 10–20 bikes**  
This is the critical bridge between prototype and volume. You want mixed adult city bikes, mostly Dutch brands, in usable condition. Target price range once you’re buying 10+ is roughly 25–60 euros per bike depending on grade and how much work you’re willing to do. Cleaner, recently ridden examples sit higher; rougher but complete ones sit lower.  

The most efficient source for this quantity is the Dutch used-bike exporters concentrated in the north (Groningen area, especially Ulrum). Used Bicycle Export and similar operations regularly hold thousands of bikes. They sell in lots starting at 10. Historical volume pricing for mixed used city bikes has sat in the low tens of euros when you take a reasonable mixed lot. They prefer full trailers or containers but will do smaller lots, especially if you present as a serious ongoing buyer.

**How to approach them**  
Call or email directly. Keep it short and concrete:

- You are a small Amsterdam project starting a free community bike fleet (modern white-bike style) at Science Park.  
- You need a test lot of 10–20 solid used city bikes now.  
- If the test works you will scale and buy larger lots regularly.  
- You will handle transport (rented van).  
- You pay quickly.  

Ask for current stock of mixed adult city bikes, price per bike at the 10–20 quantity, grading (what “usable” means to them), and whether they can set aside a cleaner selection. Mention that a successful small order will lead to bigger ones. These exporters see plenty of one-off buyers; the “test first, then volume” framing plus a real first order is what gets attention. Have the van booked or bookable so you can move fast when they say yes.

**3. Scaling steps after the first 10–20**  
Once the first batch is out and working, the numbers become easier.  

- 20–40 bikes: still in the same exporter channel. Price per bike usually drops further.  
- 40–80+: you can start talking proper volume discounts and more selective grading. At this point you can also open conversations with slightly higher-tier B2B suppliers (for example Biketraders and similar) who deal in young-used or lightly refurbished bikes if you decide you want better average condition.  
- Larger still: full trailer or small container loads become realistic. The same northern exporters can supply hundreds. Price continues to fall with quantity, though transport and your own sorting/repair time become the bigger costs.

**4. Price reality check (2026)**  
- Single local bike: 80–120 €  
- 10–20 mixed used city bikes from exporter: roughly 25–60 € each (depending on how picky you are)  
- 50+: often mid-to-low twenties or lower for mixed lots  
- Cleaner/younger used or lightly refurbished: higher, sometimes 80–150 € range depending on supplier  

These are working numbers based on the current exporter market. Always confirm live prices — stock and pricing move.

**5. Practical buying sequence**  
1. Buy and fully process the single prototype.  
2. Contact 2–3 exporters with the short test-lot pitch.  
3. Choose the best offer, rent the van, collect 10–20.  
4. Process them (paint, permanent mark, locks, Zenodo) at or near Science Park.  
5. Release and observe.  
6. Immediately go back to the same supplier (or the next) for the next doubling while the relationship is warm.  

**6. Extra levers that help**  
- Offer to take mixed sizes and a few women’s frames — exporters like clearing mixed stock.  
- Be ready to pay on collection or same-day transfer.  
- If one exporter is slow, the next one often has stock. There are several operating in the same region.  
- Once you have done two or three successful small-to-medium orders, you can start asking for first refusal on cleaner lots or slightly better grading.

That is the complete buying spine: one local prototype → 10–20 from a northern exporter with a clear “test then scale” pitch → repeated doubling using the same channel while the relationship is live. Everything else (paint, marking, locks, Zenodo, release) sits on top of this supply line.
