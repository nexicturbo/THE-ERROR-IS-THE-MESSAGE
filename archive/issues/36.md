In the Netherlands, a Stichting (Dutch foundation) is a flexible, highly effective legal entity designed to hold assets and manage projects for a specific purpose without having shareholders or traditional owners. It is widely used by civil society organizations, cultural initiatives, open-source projects (like the Blender Foundation), and family trusts.
Key Structural Characteristics
 * No Members or Shareholders: Unlike an association (vereniging) or a corporation (BV), a Stichting has no owners or members. It is a legal persona that owns itself, governed entirely by its legal purpose and board.
 * Non-Profit Restriction: A Stichting can generate revenue and make a profit, but profits cannot be distributed to founders, board members, or external investors. All earnings must be reinvested back into the entity's stated mission.
 * Governing Board: Operations are managed by a Board of Directors (bestuur). Board members can be paid reasonable administrative compensation, but they cannot receive dividend-style profit payouts.
 * Liability Protection: A Stichting is a separate legal entity (rechtspersoon). The personal assets of board members are generally protected from corporate debt, provided the board acts in good faith and avoids gross negligence or fraudulent management.
Establishment & Regulatory Requirements
 * Notarial Deed (Notariële Akte): Formation requires a Dutch public notary (notaris) to execute the deed of incorporation. This document defines the official name, registered office, statutory purpose (doel), and procedures for appointing or replacing board members.
 * Chamber of Commerce Registration: The entity must be registered with the Dutch Business Register at the Kamer van Koophandel (KvK). Until this registration is complete, board members remain personally liable for actions taken in the name of the foundation.
 * UBO Register: Foundations must identify and register their Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBOs)—typically the primary board members or individuals with significant control—in the national Dutch register.
Special Designations & Statuses
 * ANBI Status (Algemeen Nut Beogende Instelling): Foundations operating for the public good can apply for ANBI status with the Dutch Tax Authority. This makes donations tax-deductible for donors and exempts the foundation from gift and inheritance taxes.
 * SBBI Status (Sociaal Belang Behartigende Instelling): Designed for social interest organizations (like local sports clubs or neighborhood associations) that benefit a specific group rather than the general public, providing tailored tax exemptions.
