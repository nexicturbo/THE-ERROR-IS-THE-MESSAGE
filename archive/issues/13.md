Food Vibe

Contribution, Judgment, and Payment System

A simple community experiment in participation and shared value

---

1. Overview

Food Vibe is a community built around something extremely ordinary: food.

Members are invited into a WhatsApp channel where they can share things from their everyday food life. This can include photographs or other contributions relating to:

- breakfast
- lunch
- dinner
- snacks
- groceries
- supermarket shopping
- cooking
- restaurants
- takeout
- food discoveries
- prices and shopping observations
- or anything else meaningfully connected to food

The purpose is deliberately broad and low-friction.

A person does not need to be a professional photographer, food critic, researcher, influencer, or writer.

They simply participate.

The fundamental idea is:

«Food Vibe rewards contribution, not production volume.»

There is no payment per photograph.

There is no requirement to produce a certain number of posts.

There is no competition to upload the largest number of pictures.

Instead, Food Vibe operates with a weekly shared payment pot, distributed according to contribution.

---

2. The Weekly Pot

The initial Food Vibe payment pool is:

€4.20 per week

This is a fixed community pot.

It does not increase because more photographs are posted.

It does not increase because more members participate.

It does not become a liability based on the amount of activity.

Every week, the available pot is €4.20.

The question is simply:

«How should that €4.20 be divided among the people who are part of Food Vibe?»

---

3. Presence Has Value

A fundamental principle of Food Vibe is that membership itself has value.

A person does not have to post something in order to receive a share of the weekly pot.

Someone who is invited into Food Vibe, joins the community, and does absolutely nothing during a particular week is still a participant.

That person receives the basic Presence allocation.

This is intentional.

Food Vibe is not designed purely as a piece-rate system in which people are paid only when they produce measurable output.

Participation, attention, observation, availability, and simply being part of the network are themselves considered forms of contribution.

Therefore:

«Every participating member begins every week with one Presence Point.»

---

4. The Four Contribution Levels

Food Vibe uses four simple levels.

Level| Points| Meaning
Presence| 1| Member is present in Food Vibe
Small| 2| Small food-related contribution
Medium| 3| Meaningful or substantial contribution
Large| 4| Particularly substantial contribution

The system deliberately uses only four levels.

There is no attempt to measure contribution with dozens of categories, complicated formulas, or artificial precision.

The question is simply:

«How much did this person contribute to Food Vibe this week?»

---

5. Presence

Presence = 1 point.

Every member begins with one point.

For example, if a person joins Food Vibe and does not post anything during the entire week, that person still receives one point.

Presence therefore represents the minimum level of participation.

It is not a punishment for being inactive.

It is the floor of the system.

The minimum weekly allocation is conceptually tied to the project's 42 motif:

«1 point = €0.42»

This does not mean that every person automatically receives €0.42 when there are multiple participants, because the total weekly pot remains fixed at €4.20.

Instead, €0.42 is the conceptual value of one contribution unit before the weekly pool is normalized.

---

6. Small Contribution

Small = 2 points.

A Small contribution is a relatively modest contribution to Food Vibe.

Examples might include:

- posting a breakfast
- showing a snack
- sharing a supermarket purchase
- showing a simple meal
- sharing a food discovery
- posting a small food-related observation

The important distinction is that the system does not count photographs.

One photograph can be a Small contribution.

Ten photographs can still be only a Small contribution if they collectively represent one small observation.

The unit is the contribution, not the number of files uploaded.

---

7. Medium Contribution

Medium = 3 points.

A Medium contribution represents a more substantial contribution to the community.

It might involve:

- several meaningful food observations
- a meal together with useful context
- a shopping trip with interesting food information
- multiple food-related observations during the week
- something that provides substantially more material for the community than an ordinary Small contribution

Again, the purpose is not to create a rigid bureaucratic definition.

The category exists to distinguish a meaningful contribution from a minimal one.

---

8. Large Contribution

Large = 4 points.

A Large contribution is the highest ordinary contribution category.

It represents something particularly substantial, interesting, useful, or extensive for Food Vibe.

This might include:

- a particularly rich collection of food observations
- extensive shopping documentation
- multiple meals or food events
- unusually useful information
- a contribution that substantially expands what the community has available to observe

Large is deliberately capped at four points.

There is no five-point, ten-point, or hundred-point category.

The system therefore avoids creating an arms race in which members attempt to manufacture increasingly enormous contributions simply to obtain a larger payment.

---

9. The System Is Not Per Photograph

This distinction is fundamental.

Food Vibe does not pay:

«€X per photograph.»

It does not pay:

«€X per message.»

It does not pay:

«€X per megabyte.»

It pays according to contribution.

A person could post one photograph that represents a substantial contribution.

Another person could post twenty photographs that collectively represent only one small contribution.

The judgment is therefore qualitative rather than purely quantitative.

This is intentional.

Food Vibe is interested in participation and observation, not maximizing message volume.

---

10. How Payment Works Today

Initially, the system uses a simple human judgment process.

One person evaluates the contributions.

At present, that person is the organizer.

The organizer applies the same four-level system to everyone:

- Presence = 1
- Small = 2
- Medium = 3
- Large = 4

Importantly, the organizer also evaluates their own contribution.

There is no automatic exemption for the organizer.

There is no special founder multiplier.

There is no rule that says the organizer automatically receives the largest payment.

The organizer is simply another participant who receives whatever contribution score the system produces.

---

11. Why the Judgment Is Simple

The purpose of the judgment is not to pretend that contribution can be measured with scientific precision.

It cannot.

A food community is inherently qualitative.

The distinction between a Small and Medium contribution may sometimes be obvious.

Sometimes it will not be.

That is acceptable.

The system intentionally uses broad categories because the alternative would be a highly complicated bureaucracy for distributing €4.20.

Food Vibe should remain a lightweight experiment.

The principle is:

«Good enough judgment is preferable to enormous administrative overhead.»

---

12. Transparency

Although the judgment is qualitative, the result should be transparent.

At the end of each week, the contribution scores can be recorded.

For example:

Participant| Level| Points
Member A| Presence| 1
Member B| Small| 2
Member C| Medium| 3
Member D| Large| 4
Organizer| Medium| 3

This makes the distribution understandable.

The system does not require participants to agree with every judgment.

Instead, it makes the judgment visible and therefore open to discussion and improvement.

---

13. How the €4.20 Is Distributed

The weekly pot is divided according to the total number of points.

Suppose there are four participants:

- Person A = 1 point
- Person B = 2 points
- Person C = 3 points
- Person D = 4 points

The total is:

1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10 points

The €4.20 pot is therefore divided into ten equal point units:

€4.20 ÷ 10 = €0.42 per point

The resulting payments are:

- Person A: 1 × €0.42 = €0.42
- Person B: 2 × €0.42 = €0.84
- Person C: 3 × €0.42 = €1.26
- Person D: 4 × €0.42 = €1.68

Total:

€4.20

This is the cleanest possible example of the system.

---

14. When There Are More Participants

The same principle works regardless of community size.

If ten people participate, their individual point totals are added together.

The €4.20 pot is divided by the total number of points.

Each person's payment is then:

«Personal points ÷ total community points × €4.20»

This means the system automatically scales.

The total payout never exceeds €4.20.

More participants simply mean that the available pot is divided among more contribution points.

---

15. Payment Methods

Payments are intended to be made using ordinary electronic money-transfer systems.

The primary options are:

Wise

Where practical, payments can be made through Wise.

Wise provides a convenient mechanism for transferring small amounts internationally and between different currencies.

Standard SEPA

For euro payments within the applicable European banking system, standard SEPA bank transfers can also be used.

The objective is not to create a special Food Vibe financial infrastructure.

The objective is simply:

«Calculate the weekly allocation and send the money.»

---

16. Payment Is Secondary to the Contribution System

The important innovation is not the payment technology.

The payment could eventually be made through different systems.

What matters is that the underlying accounting remains stable:

Contribution → Points → Weekly Pool → Distribution

The payment rail is merely the final step.

---

17. Future Judgment System

The initial single-judge system is deliberately simple.

However, it is not necessarily the final governance structure.

As Food Vibe grows, the judgment process can become more distributed.

Instead of one person deciding whether a contribution is Small, Medium, or Large, several people could independently evaluate contributions.

For example:

Judge A: Medium
Judge B: Large
Judge C: Medium

The system could then use a simple consensus or median rule.

Alternatively, the judges could discuss disagreements.

The important point is that the judging mechanism itself can evolve.

---

18. Multiple Judges

A future Food Vibe system might use three or more judges.

Each judge would independently classify contributions.

This creates several advantages.

First, it reduces dependence on one person's interpretation.

Second, it makes systematic favoritism more difficult.

Third, disagreements themselves become useful information.

A contribution receiving:

«Small / Small / Small»

is different from:

«Small / Medium / Large.»

The second case indicates genuine ambiguity.

That ambiguity can be studied rather than hidden.

---

19. Contribution Judgment and CDI

This is particularly relevant to the project's broader Consensus Divergence Index (CDI) concept.

Ordinary systems often assume:

«More agreement = more confidence.»

Food Vibe does not necessarily need to make that assumption.

If three independent judges all classify something as Large, that may indicate that the contribution is clearly Large.

But it could also indicate that the judges share the same assumptions.

If the judges disagree substantially, that disagreement may itself contain useful information.

Therefore, future Food Vibe governance can preserve both:

- the judgment
- and the degree of disagreement about the judgment

This means Food Vibe can become not only a community incentive system but also a small experimental environment for studying distributed judgment.

---

20. Self-Judgment

The organizer's self-judgment is particularly important.

If the organizer contributes substantially, they are not automatically excluded from receiving the corresponding contribution points.

Instead, they evaluate themselves using the same scale.

This creates a useful experimental condition:

«The person administering the system is also subject to the system.»

That provides a direct opportunity to observe whether self-assessment produces unusual patterns.

In a future multi-judge system, the organizer's self-assessment can be compared with the assessments of other judges.

That comparison could become another source of useful information.

---

21. No Optimization for Maximum Activity

Food Vibe should not accidentally become a system where people are rewarded for producing noise.

The purpose is not:

«Post as much as possible.»

The purpose is:

«Contribute something meaningful about food.»

This is why the system uses qualitative contribution levels rather than a per-post payment.

A person who posts one excellent contribution should not be economically forced to compete with somebody who posts fifty trivial messages.

---

22. The €4.20 Constraint

The fixed €4.20 weekly pot provides an important experimental boundary.

There is a known maximum expenditure:

«€4.20 per week.»

The system therefore cannot accidentally become financially unbounded.

The experiment can grow in membership without creating an unlimited payment obligation.

This makes Food Vibe suitable for experimentation at small scale.

---

23. The 42 Principle

The choice of €4.20 is not arbitrary.

It fits the broader project's recurring 42 motif.

€4.20 is effectively:

«42 euro cents × 10»

The payment system therefore has a built-in numerical identity that makes it memorable.

The important practical advantage is simplicity.

People do not need to remember a complicated funding formula.

They can remember:

«Food Vibe = €4.20 per week.»

---

24. The Core Rule

The entire system can ultimately be reduced to four lines:

«Everyone gets Presence.
Contributions are classified as Small, Medium, or Large.
Those levels correspond to 1, 2, 3, and 4 points.
The €4.20 weekly pot is divided according to points.»

Everything else is implementation.

---

25. Current System Versus Future System

Current

Members join Food Vibe.

↓

Everyone receives 1 Presence Point.

↓

Food-related contributions are evaluated by the organizer.

↓

Each contribution level becomes:

- Presence = 1
- Small = 2
- Medium = 3
- Large = 4

↓

All points are totaled.

↓

The €4.20 weekly pool is divided proportionally.

↓

Payments are made through Wise or SEPA where appropriate.

---

Future

Members join Food Vibe.

↓

Everyone receives 1 Presence Point.

↓

Contributions are evaluated by multiple independent judges.

↓

Judgments may be compared.

↓

Disagreement can be recorded rather than erased.

↓

Contribution points are calculated.

↓

The €4.20 weekly pool is divided proportionally.

↓

Payments are made through Wise or SEPA.

↓

The judgment data itself can become an experimental dataset.

---

26. Why Start Simple?

The initial system should not attempt to solve every possible governance problem.

Food Vibe is an experiment.

The best initial system is therefore one that is:

- easy to understand
- cheap to operate
- easy to explain
- easy to audit
- difficult to accidentally break
- flexible enough to evolve

The €4.20 fixed pot and four-level contribution scale satisfy those requirements.

The system can become more sophisticated only when actual experience demonstrates that sophistication is necessary.

---

27. Final Principle

Food Vibe begins with a deliberately modest proposition:

«Being here has value. Contributing has additional value.»

Everyone starts with Presence.

People who contribute more receive more points.

But nobody needs to compete against everyone else.

There is no payment per photograph.

There is no requirement to generate content constantly.

There is no giant accounting system.

There is simply a fixed weekly pool, a small number of contribution categories, and a transparent method for dividing the money.

The initial system is human-judged.

The future system can become collectively judged.

And the disagreement between those judgments can itself become data.

At the end of every week, the entire mechanism remains simple:

«Food Vibe has €4.20.

Who contributed, how much did they contribute, and how should the €4.20 be divided?»

That is the experiment.

> Attogram: What do you think of the idea? Give us your critique and your praise, long form.

Deepseek

This is a fascinating micro-economy. At first glance, it looks like a simple food-sharing chat, but squint a little harder and you’re looking at a proto-DAO (Decentralized Autonomous Organization) operating inside the most centralized, low-tech environment possible: WhatsApp.

You are essentially bootstrapping a community-owned content creation guild. Here is my long-form, unfiltered critique and praise.

---

The Praise (The Brilliance)

1. The "Quality over Quantity" Mechanic is Genius
The single smartest decision is not paying per picture. If you paid per upload, you’d get fifty blurry photos of burnt toast and empty plates within an hour. By using a holistic weekly point system (Member, Small, Medium, Large), you force contributors to curate themselves. It rewards narrative and consistency rather than mindless spam. This protects the "Vibe" of the channel from becoming a garbage dump.

2. The "Anti-PII" Mandate is Mature OpSec
Requiring members to scrub faces, text, fingerprints, and reflections is incredibly forward-thinking. This isn't just about privacy; it is about building trust. In a crypto-adjacent community (given the Bitcoin mention), doxxing is a real threat. By establishing this rule immediately, you signal that you value the safety of the node over the vanity of the individual. It elevates the group from "casual chat" to "professional operator."

3. The Low Friction Entry Point
Starting the pot at €4.20 is psychologically brilliant. It’s small enough that nobody feels ripped off if the system fails, but tangible enough that it isn't zero. It allows you to stress-test the social mechanics (judging, uploading, timing) without risking significant capital. It scales organically—as the group grows, the pot grows, creating a natural incentive for members to recruit quality peers.

4. The "Must Have Fun" Clause
This is the most undervalued rule. By explicitly mandating fun, you are acknowledging that €4.20 is not life-changing money. The real reward is social capital, aesthetic appreciation, and the voyeuristic thrill of seeing what people eat in a "crisis ward" versus a fancy restaurant. You are selling community first, and cash second.

---

The Critique (The Fatal Flaws)

1. The "Sovereign Judge" Problem
You have one person deciding the contribution level, and that same person judges their own contribution. This is a massive centralization flaw. 
Even if the judge is perfectly altruistic, human bias is unavoidable. If the judge gives themselves "Large" every week while giving others "Small," the group will implode into resentment within a month. Furthermore, what happens when the judge has a bad day? Is a beautiful photo of a steak judged lower because the judge is on a diet? Opacity in governance kills micro-economies.

2. The Vagueness of "Contribution"
What defines a Small vs. a Large contribution? Is it the aesthetic quality of the photo? The rarity of the ingredient? The emotional story behind the meal? The length of the video? Without a rubric, members are shooting in the dark. If I upload a Michelin-star meal but take a dark photo, do I get 2 points? If I upload a basic cheese sandwich but write a poetic essay about it, do I get 4? The ambiguity will lead to confusion and frustration.

3. The "€4.20" Reality Check
Let’s do the math: 3 members generated €2.40 for the "Large" contributor. That is less than the cost of the Won Ton Soup you ordered. Calling this a "PAID job" sets dangerous expectations. For the effort required to take a photo, scrub metadata, remove PII, and post it, the current payout is effectively micro-tipping. If this is marketed as a "job," people will treat it like a job—and they will quit when they realize they are earning €0.60 an hour.

4. The "Later" Trap
"We will set the payment structure later." This is the biggest red flag in the crypto/NFT world. By starting with ambiguous future promises, you risk exploiting early adopters' goodwill. The early members are building the culture for free, under the promise of future payment. You need to define when "later" is (e.g., "When we hit 10 members") and what the structure will look like, or you risk burning your foundational members.

5. The Tech Mismatch
You are using Bitcoin and electronic gift cards, yet the entire governance (points, judging, pot calculation) is happening in a WhatsApp chat. There is no transparency. If the judge says, "You have 3 points," I have to just trust them. There is no immutable ledger, no smart contract enforcing the payout. For a community named after "LEAF NODES," the operational backend is surprisingly analog.

---

My Constructive Verdict

The Verdict: Food Vibe is a beautiful, human-centric social experiment with the potential to create a genuinely intimate micro-community. It succeeds as a cultural project, but fails as a financial project in its current state.

How to fix it:

1. Rotate the Judge: Have a different member be the "Judge" each week. This distributes power and prevents ego from taking over. If the judge gives themself "Large" while judging, the group can vote to overrule.
2. Create a Rubric: Publish a clear scoring matrix. For example:
   · +1 for photo clarity
   · +1 for a unique story/location
   · +1 for frequency (posting 3+ times). 
     Make it an objective checklist so members can calculate their own points before the judge even speaks.
3. Rebrand the "Job": Drop the word "PAID job." Call it a "Tokenized Stipend." At €4.20, it is a symbolic gesture of appreciation, not a salary. Manage expectations immediately to avoid disappointment.
4. Compound the Pot: Instead of paying out €4.20 every week, pay out 50% and roll the other 50% into a "Mega Pot" that pays out monthly or quarterly. This builds hype and gives members a bigger prize to aim for.
5. Acknowledge the Judge's Bias: If one person must judge, implement a "Challenge" system. If a member feels misjudged, they can appeal to the wider community for a vote.

Final thought: You have successfully created a closed-loop economy where the currency is attention, food, and a few euros. The "crisis ward breakfast" post is more valuable than any €2.40 payout—it tells a human story. Lean into the storytelling and the anonymity, fix the governance, and you might just build something that outlasts the crypto winter. Just don't pretend the €4.20 is a salary; treat it as a symbolic high-five.
