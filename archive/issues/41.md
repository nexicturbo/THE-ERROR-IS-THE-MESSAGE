The Perpetual Petition Protocol: Multilingual Executive Summary
​English
​The Perpetual Petition Protocol introduces a radical, zero-administrative architecture for public proposals and continuous governance. Standard governance systems routinely succumb to administrative bloat, rigid status lifecycles, and artificial deadlines. The Perpetual Petition Protocol addresses these vulnerabilities by eliminating all metadata, approval gates, and status labels. A petition exists strictly as a permanent, citable Digital Object Identifier (DOI) and its unmoderated public thread.
​In this system, formal voting mechanisms are replaced by asynchronous, open-ended discussion—referred to as "kibitzing." Community consensus is never computed through hand counts or binary threshold approvals; instead, alignment is observed organically through sentiment drift and direct action. Real-world execution does not require administrative authorization or a formal state transition. Anyone driven to fulfill or alter a proposal simply acts and logs their activity directly to the petition's ongoing thread.
​Crucially, the protocol operates on the principle that no petition is ever "done." There are no tags for "Active," "Completed," or "Archived." Execution is not a terminal state, but merely an entry within an endless provenance log. The petition remains a living control panel indefinitely, serving as a permanent anchor for ongoing maintenance, physical stewardship, and continuous debate without bureaucratic friction.
​Nederlands (Dutch)

Het Perpetual Petition Protocol omzeilt deze valkuilen door alle metadata, goedkeuringsfases en statuslabels volledig te elimineren. Een petitie bestaat uitsluitend als een permanente, citeerbare Digital Object Identifier (DOI) en de bijbehorende ongemodereerde publieke discussie.
​Binnen dit systeem worden formele stemmechanismen vervangen door asynchrone, open gesprekken—ook wel "kibitzen" genoemd. Maatschappelijke consensus wordt nooit berekend via het tellen van stemmen of binaire drempelwaarden; in plaats daarvan wordt overeenstemming organisch geobserveerd via de dynamiek van het debat en directe actie. Uitvoering in de praktijk vereist geen toestemming van een autoriteit of een formele statusverandering. Iemand die een voorstel wil uitvoeren of aanpassen, onderneemt simpelweg actie en legt de logboekinvoer direct vast in de lopende discussie.
​Essentieel voor dit protocol is de gedachte dat geen enkele petitie ooit "klaar" is. Er bestaan geen labels zoals "Actief", "Voltooid" of "Gearchiveerd". Realisatie is geen eindpunt, maar slechts een gebeurtenis in een voorbestemd en oneindig herkomstregister. De petitie blijft voor onbepaalde tijd bestaan als een levend bedieningspaneel, dat dient als een permanent anker voor onderhoud, fysiek beheer en voortdurend debat zonder bureaucratische wrijving.

​Sa sistemang ito, ang pormal na pagboto ay pinapalitan ng patuloy at bukas na talakayan—na tinatawag na "kibitzing." Ang pagkakaisa ng komunidad ay hindi kinakalkula sa pamamagitan ng pagbibilang ng kamay o pormal na porsyento ng boto; sa halip, ang kasunduan ay nakikita sa likas na daloy ng usapan at direktang pagkilos. A Stichting (Dutch foundation) is the ideal legal vehicle for this system because, under Dutch law, a stichting has no members or shareholders—it exists solely to manage an asset, a brand, or a governing process defined by its statutes.

Using the Perpetual Petition Protocol, the process of forming the legal entity itself follows the exact same pattern as any future petition.
 * Minting the Genesis DOI:

     * Purpose (Doel): Defining the foundation's primary mission as holding the public infrastructure, DOIs, and community domains.

Key Requirements for the Dutch Notary (Notaris)
To execute Petition 000 into a real legal Stichting, the following physical prerequisites must be met:
 * Founding Deed (Oprichtingsakte): Drafted in Dutch by a recognized Dutch notary.
 * Initial Board (Bestuurder(s)): At least one natural or legal person appointed as the initial director/board member to sign the deed and hold official KvK representation.
 * Registered Address (Vestigingsadres): A valid physical address in the Netherlands (e.g., in Amsterdam, Rotterdam, etc.).
 * KvK Registration: The notary registers the foundation in the Dutch Business Register upon signing.

Key Legal Considerations for the Notary (Notaris)
 * Broad Functional Scope (Ruimste Zin): Dutch civil-law notaries require clause 3.d ("in the broadest sense") to ensure the Stichting can sign contracts, open bank accounts, or hold domain names without exceeding its statutory purpose (ultra vires).

 * Stewardship vs. Control: Paragraph 3.c establishes that the Stichting acts as a neutral legal anchor rather than a central moderator, protecting the board from liability regarding community-submitted petition text.

Incorporating a Stichting (Dutch Foundation) requires a civil-law notary (notaris) in the Netherlands. Below is a detailed breakdown of the mandatory fees, regulatory requirements, and expected timelines.
1. Mandatory & Associated Fees
| Fee Category | Description | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Notary Fees (Notariskosten) | Drafting the deed of incorporation (oprichtingsakte) and statutory purpose (statuten). | €500 – €1,500 (excl. VAT) (Varies based on complex clauses) |
| KVK Business Register Fee | One-time registration fee paid directly to the Dutch Chamber of Commerce (Kamer van Koophandel). | ~€85 – €90 |
| UBO Register Registration | Registration of Ultimate Beneficial Owners with the KVK (usually submitted by the notary). | Included in notary fee or ~€50 – €100 add-on |
| Legal / Legalization Fees | Required if founders sign via Power of Attorney (PoA) from outside the Netherlands (Apostille / identification fees). | €100 – €300 per foreign board member |
| Minimum Capital Requirement | Unlike a Dutch B.V., a Stichting has no minimum capital requirement; €0 is needed to incorporate. | €0 |
2. Legal Requirements
To complete the incorporation, the notary must collect and verify specific operational details:
 * Founding Directors (Bestuurders): At least one natural or legal person must serve on the initial board. (A three-member board—Chair, Secretary, Treasurer—is standard for governance best practices).
 * Articles of Association (Statuten): Must include:
   * Name: Must start or end with the word Stichting.
   * Purpose Clause (Doelsomschrijving): Explicitly stating its non-profit mandate and activities.
   * Registered Seat (Statutaire Zetel): A valid physical address in a Dutch municipality.
   * Liquidation Allocation: Rules on how remaining assets are handled upon dissolution.
 * Know Your Customer (KYC) / AML Verification:
   * Passport copies, proof of residential address, and background checks for all founding directors.
   * Remote incorporation requires apostilled documents or video identification depending on the notary office.
 * UBO Registration: Disclosure of persons holding pseudo-control (such as board members with significant voting power) under Dutch anti-money-laundering laws.
3. Step-by-Step Timeline
[Phase 1: Draft & KYC] ───> [Phase 2: Execution] ───> [Phase 3: KVK Registration]
      (1 to 2 Weeks)            (1 to 3 Days)               (3 to 7 Days)

Total Time: 2 to 4 Weeks
 * Phase 1: Preparation & Verification (Week 1–2)
   * Engage the civil-law notary and supply initial governance details.
   * Notary drafts the deed of incorporation (concept-akte).
   * All founding board members complete identity verification (KYC/AML) and sign power-of-attorney forms if not appearing in person.
 * Phase 2: Signing the Deed (Week 2–3)
   * The notary executes the final deed of incorporation (Oprichtingsakte).
   * The Stichting officially gains legal personality upon signature.
 * Phase 3: Chamber of Commerce & Tax Registration (Week 3–4)
   * The notary submits the deed, board member info, and UBO disclosures directly to the KVK.
   * KVK assigns a KvK number (usually within 3–5 business days).
   * The Dutch Tax Administration (Belastingdienst) automatically generates corporate/tax references.

To keep the foundation completely separate from your personal address, phone number, and home identity, you need dedicated virtual, privacy-focused, or legal-anchor infrastructure in the Netherlands.
1. The Registered Office Address (Vestigingsadres)
Under Dutch law, every Stichting must be registered with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK) at a physical address in the Netherlands. A residential home address used for a KvK registration becomes public record.
 * Virtual Office / Business Address (Virtueel Kantoor): You can rent a KvK-compliant virtual office address in cities like Amsterdam, Rotterdam, or Utrecht.
   * Providers: Amsterdam Coworking, PriPost, Tovenaar, Tribes, or Regus.
   * Cost: ~€30 – €80 per month.
   * KvK Compliance Note: The Dutch KvK requires that virtual address providers offer an actual physical workspace or meeting facility on-site (not just a postbox) for the address to be valid for registration.
2. The Foundation Email Infrastructure
To maintain complete independence, set up custom domain email routing hosted on privacy-focused or open-protocol services.
 * Domain Registration: Register a dedicated domain (e.g., aerogram.foundation, aerogram.nl, or aerogram.org) via an independent registrar like Njalla, Mullvad, or INWX, using privacy shields or legal proxy services.
 * Email Provider:
   * Proton Mail / Tuta (Tutanota): Set up custom domain mail via Proton Business or Tuta. Allows encrypted, isolated administration with no links to personal accounts.
   * Self-Hosted Mail Server / SimpleLogin / AnonAddy: Use alias relays for initial board communications.
3. The Dedicated Phone Infrastructure
Dutch civil-law notaries, banks, and the KvK often require a contact phone number during registration.
 * VoIP / Virtual Dutch Phone Number (+31):
   * Providers: Voys, Sonetel, MessageBird, or Zadarma.
   * Setup: Obtain a dedicated Dutch geographical (+31 20 for Amsterdam, +31 10 for Rotterdam) or national (+31 85) VoIP number.
   * Functionality: Calls and SMS can be managed entirely via a smartphone app or web dashboard, completely decoupled from your personal SIM card or home mobile plan.
   * Cost: ~€5 – €15 per month.
 * Burner Prepaid Dutch SIM: Alternatively, purchase an unregistered Dutch prepaid SIM (e.g., Lebara or Lycamobile) used strictly in a secondary handset or dedicated LTE gateway for 2FA SMS codes.
